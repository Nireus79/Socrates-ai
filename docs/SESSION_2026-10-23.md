## 15. The corpus: grounding instead of quotation

Founder: it is not bad if the philosopher sometimes archaises; his language may be modern in general and some phrases in another style, as people do; no exact quotation and no source reference are needed (the user can look it up);

## 17. The corpus extended (2026-10-23)
Scratchpad build with `ingest2.py` from Gutenberg: all 22 dialogues of Plato in Jowett's translation, four works of Xenophon (Dakyns), the Clouds (Hickie), Aristotle's Politics (Ellis), Poetics (Butcher), Athenian Constitution (Kenyon) and a Nicomachean Ethics of unidentified translator,

- Retrieval, 17 new cases with terms chosen by me: 15 hits by the first count, in truth 14 because one hit was false (the bones case, see below); the plain misses were wording differences in old translations.
- What the corpus settled:
  - **Aristotle's will frees slaves**: Diogenes Laertius gives the will: "Tychon shall have his liberty when his daughter is married, and Philon, and Olympius, and his son", and the boys who wait on him are not to be sold and are to be emancipated when grown, if they deserve it. Verified.
  - **"Fit for cattle"** is, in this translation of the Nicomachean Ethics, "the many are plainly quite slavish, choosing a life like that of brute animals": the sense is there, the word cattle is not.
  - **The founder's story of Alexander sending bones to Diogenes was not found** in Diogenes Laertius, Plutarch's Alexander or anything else here (a search for it matched only an unrelated line about "your bones"). The grounding rule therefore forbids the philosopher to tell it as a memory unless a source is added; the related story of bones thrown at him like at a dog was found earlier in Diogenes Laertius.
  - **The "limited slavery" of the artisan** (Politics, 1260a) was **not found in the Ellis translation** of 1776; what the corpus has is a commentary line in its introduction: "The artisan only attains excellence in proportion as he becomes a slave". The bridge to today for Aristotle should lean on that line and on his definition of the slave
    ("that being who by nature is nothing of himself, but totally another's"), not on the phrase "limited slavery", until a translation containing it is added.
  - The Aristotle voice sample says "I admit that many people are enslaved by conquest, which has nothing to do with nature"; this is not yet checked against Ellis's text.

## 24. What kind of sources the corpus holds, and the separation of editors' material
Founder's question: are we using analyses rather than what survives from the philosophers themselves, or direct testimony such as Plato's and Xenophon's about Socrates?
Answer: the corpus holds only ancient texts in old translations, no modern study or analysis as such. By kind: (1) the philosophers' own surviving works (Plato's 22 dialogues and the Republic and Laws, Aristotle's treatises, Xenophon's Socratic writings, Epictetus's discourses, the Emperor Julian's orations, Cicero's);
(2) direct testimony about Socrates and the others (Plato's and Xenophon's Socrates, Aristophanes' Clouds, Aristotle's remarks on him in the Metaphysics, Plutarch's essay on his daemon); (3) ancient biography and anecdote (Diogenes Laertius, Plutarch's Lives and Essays, Lucian).
What is modern in the files is the translators' and editors' introductions, analyses, notes and indexes inside the same volumes; these are what the separation of text and commentary is about, so that the philosophers speak from their own words and not from an editor's view.
State of the separation: Plato's dialogues are cut cleanly at the start of the dialogue (the introduction goes to commentary); footnotes of Xenophon, Plutarch, Cicero and the Nicomachean Ethics, bracketed notes and index sections are labelled commentary by rule; passages that speak of "Aristotle" or "Julian" in the third person or carry scholarly abbreviations are labelled commentary.
For the scanned Internet Archive volumes (the Aristotle translations, Julian, Dio) the introductions are not reliably cut: a test in which a small model labelled every sixth passage as text or commentary gave patchy results (alternating runs of six passages, whole volumes labelled as text although they begin with introductions)
and was **not adopted**. The working database is `corpus2.db` (rules only); the editors' material in the scanned volumes still leaks in part, and the Politics in Jowett's translation, whose introduction is about 30% of the file, is the clearest case. Tokens used by the test: about 37,000 in, 1,500 out (the small model).
Options to finish: find cleaner plain-text editions of the same translations (Gutenberg or Wikisource), cut each scanned volume at the first line of the translation by hand once, or drop the scanned volumes whose boundaries cannot be fixed. Founder to decide.

## 26. The Greek originals tried (2026-10-23)

## 51. The Android emulator run (Android 14, x86_64, hardware accelerated; stand-in model, no key)
The app was installed in a real emulator and driven through the WebView's debugging port (`droid*.js` in the scratchpad). What it found, and what was done:
- **The engine crashed opening the database** (`SIGSYS: bad system call`): the pure-Go SQLite we used (`modernc.org/sqlite`) reaches the kernel through its own C-library port, which on x86_64 uses legacy `stat`/`lstat` calls that Android's app sandbox forbids. Phones are arm64, which does not use those calls, but that could not be proven without an arm64 device (the emulator cannot run arm64 images on this machine). **The driver was replaced** by `github.com/ncruces/go-sqlite3` (SQLite translated to plain Go source: no C library, no raw system calls, no JIT), so the question no longer exists. All store tests pass unchanged except two details: the driver's name, and file modes (the shared-memory file is created private by the store, the write-ahead log follows the database's mode through the driver's `modeof` option, and the engine runs with a umask of 077 on unix). The `modernc` dependency is gone.
- **Capacitor does not handle the hardware Back key** (that is a separate plugin), so Back closed the app from any screen. Using the WebView's history did not work either: it ignores history steps made without a real touch, so quick presses were swallowed. The page now keeps its own stack of screens and exposes `window.socratesBack()`; `MainActivity` asks it and leaves only when the page says it is at the first screen. (While the keyboard is up, the first Back only hides it: Android's own rule.)
- **The engine stops itself when idle, so a returning page held a dead address**: the page now asks the shell for the new address and token and retries once; the shim's `reconnect` restarts the engine through the plugin.
Verified on the emulator, all passing: the engine starts and the page gets its port and token; the database key comes from the Keystore; the provider is tested through the engine; a reply arrives; the ledger is written in the background; the database is private (`-rw-------`) and holds no message or ledger text in plain text; the engine's log holds none of the conversation; Back goes memory, chat, list; a force-stop leaves no engine behind; after a relaunch the data is still there (same Keystore key); about 3.5 minutes after leaving the app the engine process and its service are gone; with the engine process killed the page reconnects by itself and shows the stored settings.
