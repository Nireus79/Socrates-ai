Free beta of Socrates, an app for talking with four artificial Greek philosophers: Socrates, Diogenes, Plato and Aristotle. You need your own Claude or OpenAI key. Everything is stored encrypted on your device; there is no Socrates server, no account and no analytics.

## What changed since beta.5
- New: a macOS build (`Socrates.app`, universal). It is ad-hoc signed only, not notarised, and the author has not yet run it on a Mac.
- The sponsor card can now be updated by the project without a new version. It is labelled "Sponsored", shows who made the app ("Created by Hermes Software") and links to sponsoring, the author's GitHub and Hermes Software. You can hide it.
- OpenAI: the philosophers' replies now use the small "mini" model instead of the flagship (faster and cheaper on your own key). The flagship is used only if your key has no mini. Claude is unchanged. The voices have not been measured on OpenAI models yet.
- A terms of use page on the website, linked from the navigation.

## Downloads
- `socrates-v0.1.0-beta.6-android.apk` signed Android app (Android 7 or newer, arm64). Closing the app stops its engine.
- Linux, Windows and macOS builds, unsigned or ad-hoc signed. Check a download with the `.sha256` file next to it.

## Know before you install
- Windows: the first start shows a SmartScreen warning because the build is not signed ("More info", then "Run anyway"). It needs the WebView2 runtime (included in Windows 11). The author has run the Windows build once, on 4 November.
- macOS: the app is not notarised, so macOS will warn on the first start (right-click the app, then Open). **It has not been run on a Mac by the author yet.**
- Linux: needs GTK 3 and WebKitGTK 4.1 (`libgtk-3-0`, `libwebkit2gtk-4.1-0`).
- The philosophers are artificial characters made from what was written about them. They are not real people and not a source of medical, legal or other professional advice. The app is for people aged 16 and over. If you are thinking of hurting yourself, please call your local emergency number or a crisis line.
- How well they talk, and how reliably the safety checks work, depend on the model behind them. You pay your AI provider for what you use; set a spending limit.
